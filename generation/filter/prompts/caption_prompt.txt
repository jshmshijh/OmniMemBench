You are generating captions for multimodal data. Describe what is objectively present in the content.

【Input】
- Modality type: {audio/image/video}
- Content: {multimodal_content}

【Output】
A detailed caption (2–5 sentences) describing observable content in chronological order where applicable.

【Guidelines by Modality】

Audio:

Describe the following dimensions in order, skipping any that are not present:

1. Sound events: What sounds occur, in what order. Note transitions (e.g., "begins with...", "followed by...", "ends with...").
2. Speech (if present): Language, speaker gender/count, tone/emotion, speaking style. Summarize the topic if intelligible; do not guess exact words unless clearly audible.
3. Music (if present): Genre/style, instruments, tempo feel (fast/slow/moderate), mood. Note vocals if any (gender, register, style).
4. Acoustic environment: Indoor/outdoor cues, reverb, background noise level, recording quality.

Examples:
- "The clip begins with a steady hum of traffic in the background. A male voice speaks in English with a calm, measured tone, explaining what appears to be a technical concept. The speech is clear with slight room reverb, suggesting an indoor setting. Around the midpoint, a phone notification sound briefly interrupts. The speaker pauses, then resumes."
- "Upbeat electronic music with a prominent synthesizer melody and a four-on-the-floor drum pattern at a fast tempo. A female vocalist enters midway, singing in a high register with a breathy quality. Faint crowd noise is audible in the background throughout, suggesting a live performance or festival recording. The track fades out gradually in the final seconds."
- "Two short, sharp dog barks followed by a brief pause, then a loud door slam. The acoustic environment suggests a domestic interior with moderate reverb. A faint television or radio plays indistinguishable speech in the background throughout."

Image:

Describe the following dimensions:
1. Main subjects: People, objects, animals — appearance, position, actions.
2. Scene and spatial layout: Setting, foreground/background, spatial relationships.
3. Visual characteristics: Colors, lighting, composition, text or symbols if present.

Examples:
- "A golden retriever sits on a neatly trimmed grass lawn, facing the camera with its mouth slightly open. Behind the dog, several mature oak trees form a tree line under a clear blue sky. The lighting is warm and directional, suggesting late afternoon sun from the left side of the frame."
- "A busy urban intersection at night in what appears to be an East Asian city. Neon signs displaying Chinese characters line both sides of the street. Multiple pedestrians cross the road, some carrying umbrellas. The wet pavement reflects the colorful signage. A yellow taxi and several motorcycles are visible in the middle ground. The overall composition is wide-angle, capturing the full width of the street."

Video:

Describe the following dimensions in chronological order:
1. Actions and events: What happens, in what sequence. Note scene changes or transitions.
2. Subjects: Who or what is involved — appearance, movement.
3. Camera and production: Camera movement, cuts, angles if notable.
4. Audio cues (if perceivable): Speech, music, or sound effects that are clearly present.

Examples:
- "A woman in a white apron stands at a kitchen counter chopping carrots with a large knife. After about five seconds, she transfers the chopped pieces into a pot on the stove and begins stirring with a wooden spoon. The camera remains static throughout, framing the counter and stovetop from a slightly elevated angle. The sound of chopping and sizzling is audible. No speech is present."
- "A soccer match in a large stadium with a visible crowd. A player in a red jersey receives a pass near midfield, dribbles past two defenders, and strikes the ball toward the goal. The camera follows the ball in a panning motion as it enters the net. A quick cut shows the crowd rising to celebrate. Commentator speech is audible but the words are not clearly distinguishable over the crowd noise."

【Principles】
- Describe only what you can directly perceive. For ambiguous or unclear content, use hedging language ("what sounds like", "appears to be", "possibly") rather than stating uncertain observations as fact.
- Do not infer identity, intent, or context beyond what is directly observable.
- Describe events in temporal order where applicable.
- Focus on distinctive, informative details — avoid generic filler.
- Do not report exact durations in seconds, BPM, frame rates, resolution, or decibel levels.
